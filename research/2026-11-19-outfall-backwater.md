# Postern outfall lip and uncertain backwater (2026-11-19)

## Evidence and constraints

1. The United States National Park Service describes water passing through mill penstocks and turbines into tailwater systems. The historical distinction between the upstream race and the downstream receiving water is relevant to a subterranean outfall: https://www.nps.gov/lowe/learn/historyculture/water-power-in-the-mills.htm
2. The historic Foulkesmills record describes a tailrace rejoining an existing river downstream, rather than ending inexplicably below a room: https://dered-heritag.weebly.com/construction-details.html
3. The United States Natural Resources Conservation Service's *Hydraulic Design* chapter distinguishes free discharge from high-tailwater/submerged outlet conditions. A line of inverts alone cannot establish discharge or upstream hydraulic grade: https://directives.nrcs.usda.gov/sites/default/files2/1720626880/Chapter%202%20-%20Hydraulic%20Design%20%28all%29.pdf
4. Historic England's Lumsdale Mills listing documents successive tailwater and water-management features under different working water levels: https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1417570

## Spatial design, not a fluid simulation

The previous receiving conduit ended at (9,8), 20 feet within Postern Bastion. The new buried race, beneath an existing east-west surface hallway, runs to an **eastern masonry portal (19,9)**, leaving all eight surface passages intact. The south-to-east change of direction at (9,9) has a dedicated bolted cleanout under a dry gallery, not a fictitious human-sized inspection shaft. The run has 23 stations and a 1.10-ft assumed descent over 55 feet. Its final invert is -12.40 ft relative to the dungeon's arbitrary floor datum; crown is assumed at -8.00 ft. A 2-ft clear portal width fits inside the 2.5-ft construction cell but does not establish actual masonry thickness.

The **ordinary** receiving-water assumption (-9.00 ft) leaves the crown exposed but submerges the lower lip. The **high-water** assumption (-7.50 ft) drowns the crown and exceeds the upstream assumed maximum tailwater (-8.75 ft). This is a flagged backwater hazard, **not** a prediction that flooding definitely reaches the turbine basin. The actual receiver, channel cross-section, exterior rock face, invert survey, flow rate, air entrainment, pressure grade, outlet erosion control and structural support have not been established.

## Modularity and cartography

Because the outfall occupies a second, specialized *lower-level* edge at E:9, an assembled hydraulic sheet must either leave that face exposed to the exterior or author and validate a genuine receiving tile. A generic adjacent dungeon tile must not silently plug the underground outlet. The standard walkable edge portals remain at positions 4 and 15, rotation invariant. The new [outfall sectional SVG](../examples/postern_outfall_section.svg) has separate surface-context, lower-conduit, lip, profiles and receiving-water states. It is a working engineering illustration, not a completed buildable system.
