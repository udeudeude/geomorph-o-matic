# Three authored wide-cave vaults and registered cross-sections
November 20, 2026 · fictional design study

## Sources and geological constraints

1. Waters, Donnelly-Nolan and Rogers, USGS Bulletin 1673, *Selected Caves and Lava-Tube Systems in and near Lava Beds National Monument, California* (1990): https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/b1673 . Its description of Post Office Cave distinguishes vertically stacked tubes, collapse connections and benches rather than a single uniform cylindrical void. The text describes tube cross sections changing abruptly in shape, including an A-frame form near lava benches. We do not adopt the report's measured dimensions as our fictional cave measurements.
2. The TubeX field campaign (2017-2018) and research paper, *Resolution of Lava Tubes With Ground Penetrating Radar* (2020): https://doi.org/10.1029/2019JE006138 . Observed Skull Cave and Valentine Cave geometries differ strongly: approximately 10-20 m versus 1-3 m in diameter in the article's studied reaches. Laser-survey comparisons and nonplanar voids argue against assuming a uniform ceiling from a floor plan.
3. National Park Service, Lava Beds National Monument caving descriptions: https://www.nps.gov/labe/planyourvisit/caving.htm . Naturally occurring standing-to-crawl passages provide a reason to preserve low-roof points rather than uniformly raising all ceilings.

## Three actual changes to the dungeon architecture

**Braided Lava:** a 9-foot two-cell crown at the authentic central confluence, wrapped by a separately modeled 8.5-foot shoulder, plus a lateral 8.5-foot western relict blister. The changes are made to occupied floor cells in the source map. The remaining roof and mouth heights remain in their previous zones. A restricted low-roof feeder is retained at 3.5 feet.

**Split-Spine:** an 8.5-foot two-cell northern blister arch with 8-foot surrounding shoulders, plus a separate lower 8-foot crown over the southern drainback. The central unwalkable basalt spine is unchanged. The low western septum pinch remains 3.5 feet.

**Registered transverse architectural sections:** actual west-to-east walking lines on rows 9, 12 and 4 respectively of the three source plans. Every station is a genuine occupied cell with its floor and roof z elevation derived directly from the source model; there is no invented passage across empty rock. The Sheetflow section crosses two real 1-foot double-riser stairs while its middle floor is at z=1. Horizontal and vertical scales within each section agree. Green roof lines interpolate between discrete 2.5-foot station samples; they are design envelopes, **not** measured stone surfaces.

## Audit and design critique

All three tiles have exterior floor z=0, ceiling z=6.5 feet across each protected five-foot mouth and for five feet inward. Except intentional low-roof squeezes, a maximum 0.5-foot roof-height difference is allowed between adjacent occupied cells, a deliberately conservative **drawing convention**, not an empirically established volcanic gradient or engineering code. A primary and an independent test implementation verify the source cells, noninvented traversals, preservation of openings, registered step edges, and physically separate floor/ceiling coordinates. Vertical datum is not rock support: nothing here measures overburden thickness, curvature between samples, roof bearing, stability or human safety.

The other notable limitation is that source geometry is based on a coarse 2.5-foot cell plan, so fractional wall and roof irregularity below that scale cannot yet be represented by the occupancy model. Future work should study the actual surveyed plans and longitudinal sections in Bulletin 1673 plate 5 and analogous published cave sections, then commission individually authored irregular wall/ceiling envelopes and compare on true-size paper. Treat the SVG drawings as **unfinished architectural studies**.
