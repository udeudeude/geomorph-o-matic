# Fallen Choir: the collapse is now architecture, not decoration
19 November 2026

## References and design criteria

1. [Dyson Logos, Geomorph Mapping Challenge #38 (2010)](https://dysonlogos.blog/2010/03/02/geomorph-mapping-challenge-38/) intentionally places balconies above a central room without a conveniently invented direct route into it. Distinct vertical circulation can be a compelling spatial problem; we instead provide **two explicit ladders**, tested as navigation edges.
2. [Dyson Logos, Vault of the Cave Morphs (2018)](https://dysonlogos.blog/2018/06/25/vault-of-the-cave-morphs/) keeps compatible portals while varying passage morphology. We preserve the eight project-standard exterior connection cells and loosen the old western ambulatory's rigid straight line.
3. [Dunbrody Abbey architectural description](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunbrody_Abbey) documents upper passages over church side chapels and circular stairs accessing upper storeys. It supports the *idea* of elevated church circulation, not the dimensions of our invented abbey.
4. [Mammoth Cave National Park, cave mapping](https://www.nps.gov/maca/learn/cave-mapping.htm) illustrates why changes in level should be recorded as navigable relationships, not decorative stair icons.

## Three actual changes

- **Blocked fallen arcade:** Five adjoining edge pairs through the nave are impassable at ground level. The vector renderer produces fractured, dark masonry strokes on exactly those edges; neither tiny furniture nor attractive rubble marks are falsely described as collision geometry. The lower route remains possible through the narrow surviving southern bay.
- **Navigable upper triforium:** Four overhead cells remain connected inside one architectural volume, served by two distinct ladders. The upper shortcut crosses the collapse while the lower route detours around it. A new validation rule rejects galleries passing through lower-room boundaries with no modeled upper doorway.
- **Comparison artwork and proof:** The editable two-view drawing isolates the elevated floor against a ghosted composite plan, with provisional rather than invented height dimensions. Regenerated assembled and actual-size letter proofs remain in the workflow for visual evaluation.

The geometry is original; the references are compositional and architectural precedents, not traced designs.

## Unresolved design and usability issues

The lower debris wall is a plan-level obstruction. Its actual thickness, load path, and relation to the intact triforium have not been structurally determined. The projected gallery may need stronger masonry piers or an offset to clear the enormous fallen bell. The original surrounding passages remain partly schematic. At actual print size, inspect whether the five short jagged obstruction strokes read as one coherent collapsed arcade rather than five independent wall fragments. No physical print or tabletop test has been completed.

A good next study is a **natural cavern geomorph with asymmetrical split elevations**, keeping the same seam contract but refusing the temptation to impose an abbey-like rectangular footprint.
