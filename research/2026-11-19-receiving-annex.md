# Receiving Ravine: specialist outlet annex (2026-11-19)

## Why a specialist annex rather than a generic seventh tile

The existing Postern Bastion outfall occupies the *lower* eastern edge at
E:9 and the eight standard surface entrances at positions 4 and 15 remain
untouched. Attaching an arbitrary full dungeon tile would bury the outfall
inside its walls. A receiving ravine therefore uses an explicitly specialized,
matched W:9 lower edge; it **cannot** be substituted into the normal 3x3
interchangeability test. The new validator checks the matching position under
all four quarter-turn rotations, but is not yet a general mixed-annex assembler.

## Historical and engineering evidence

1. Historic England's [Torr Vale Mill listing](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1119721)
   documents a physical ensemble of tailrace arch, watercourse wall, sluices
   and retaining walls. This supports drawing the complete masonry throat
   and retaining-cheek relationship rather than an open pipe ending in a void.
2. Historic England's [Coulton Mill listing](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1296371)
   records a stone-lined tailrace and culvert beneath a road, demonstrating
   that the underground-to-exterior transition needs distinct structural
   treatment.
3. [Historic England: Lumb Clough smeltmill](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1015819)
   preserves both buried and standing waterworks, including the tailrace
   rejoining a downstream beck.
4. The United States Bureau of Reclamation's
   [Hydraulic Design of Stilling Basins and Energy Dissipators](https://www.usbr.gov/tsc/techreferences/hydraulics_lab/pubs/EM/EM25.pdf)
   documents how downstream rock protection and energy dissipation depend
   on actual hydraulic conditions. Its design numbers must **not** be
   transplanted into this unknown small subterranean watercourse.

## Authored geometry and unresolved parameters

The exterior drawing allocates twenty sequential half-square stations
(2.5 feet each), starting at its W:9 mouth and ending at E:9. The
**centerline** is nominally straight to preserve a verifiable downhill
profile and facilitate cleaning. The outlet lip has -12.40-ft assumed invert,
the annex begins at -12.45 ft, and its final station is -13.40 ft, all
relative to an arbitrary dungeon floor datum. The channel expands
from a 2-ft masonry throat (stations 0-2), through a 3-ft flagstone apron
(3-7), 5-ft desilting reach (8-11) with an assumed 1-ft extra pocket
depth, and 7.5-ft rock-lined receiving bed (12-19).

A dry terrace, accessed from the north face of the specialist annex,
reaches an inspection landing by the pocket. A short elevated crossing
at station 12 has its deck **assumed** at -5.50 ft, 2 feet above
the high-water envelope (-7.50 ft); bank top is -6.50 ft. These are
cartographic relationships, not accessibility or flood-safety certificates.
The annex has no asserted ordinary surface portals and is not a standard
geomorph. The ordinary -9.00-ft and high -7.50-ft scenarios were inherited
from the Postern study, not predicted by a hydraulic model.

## Constraints / next research

- **Survey** a plausible exterior rock face and downstream river bed,
  rather than extrapolating a 50-ft straight channel to infinity.
- **Calculate** actual discharge, approach velocity, head losses,
  submerged outlet backwater and the required apron/apron-sill protection
  only after measured cross-sections and a tailwater rating exist.
- **Detail** the desilting-pit access ladder, bridge foundations,
  retaining wall bond, sediment removal strategy and realistic rock
  clearances. The drawn terrace is currently a connectivity study.
- **Develop** a configurable specialist-annex assembler, preserving all
  standard pedestrian portals and blocking mismatched subfloor edges.
- **Physically print** the joined plan at intended scale and inspect
  hatching, line weights, rooms and rock contours in context.

**Status:** Spatial architecture and conditional elevations are authored
and independently tested. No hydraulic or geotechnical construction
verification has been performed. Dungeon maps remain unfinished.
