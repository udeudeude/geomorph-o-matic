# Rock ribs and branching passages: two cave studies
19 November 2026 | Provisional original map revisions

## Research and geological constraints

[National Park Service: Solution Caves](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/caves/solution-caves.htm) describes tributary, joining passages formed as water enters fractured soluble rock from multiple locations. That supports a branched, sometimes constricted limestone passage, but does **not** make arbitrary decorative wiggles geologically convincing.

[National Park Service: Lava Caves](https://home.nps.gov/subjects/caves/lava-caves-or-tubes.htm) distinguishes lava channels roofed over by their own crust from limestone dissolution, noting splits, complex junctions and occasional connected levels in volcanic tubes. We therefore **do not** pretend Basalt Rift is a limestone solution cave: its long central tube remains the most coherent volcanic feature, while two upper side passages now skirt solid basalt ribs. The southern spur is a fictional surviving piece of the lava-tube roof/flow, not a measured fault block.

[National Park Service: Cave Mapping](https://home.nps.gov/maca/learn/cave-mapping.htm) explains the role of surveyed passage widths, cross-sections and profiles. The [International Union of Speleology symbol guidance](https://uis-speleo.org/index.php/mapping-symbols/) is useful for symbol hierarchy. Our values are **authored plan estimates**, not site observations or cave-survey grades.

## Change 1: Hollow Causeway's east relict channel

The formerly uninterrupted east-side hall strip ran 13 consecutive 2.5-foot half-cells (**32.5 feet**) beside the pearled pocket. The revised route now passes east around rock at `13,10`, through `14,9`-`14,11`, returning west to `13,11`. The old cell is genuine rock, not a line drawn over traversable floor. The longest hall strip is now **8 half-cells (20 feet)**. The bend is a **2.5-foot** wide constriction in plan, spreading to **5 feet** at the return; no roof or body-clearance claim is made. The actual water, bridge, upper calcite arch, two ladders, collapse choke, concealed pocket and eight standard entrances are unchanged.

## Change 2: Basalt Rift upper splays and southern approach

The previous two uniform 30-foot transverse strips across rows 9 and 17 were conspicuously diagrammatic. New offset branches cross `7,8`-`9,8` and `12,8`-`14,8` while leaving genuine basalt at `8,9` and `13,9`. The southern route skirts the retained spur at `7,17` via `6,18`-`8,18`. These are traversable floor edits, not contour-noise effects. The longest straight hall strip drops from **12 to 10 half-cells (30 to 25 feet)**, preserving the main linear tube as a deliberate geological reading. The three new constriction cross-cuts measure 2.5 feet in the floor plan. Every modular edge stays exact.

## Testing and review gates

The new regression tests require the ribs to be actual unoccupied rock and the bypasses to be hall floor, verify dry route connections through the revised approaches, preserve eight rotation-compatible `4,15` openings, guard upper bounds for straight floor strips, and check clearances at five registered transverse sections.

**Not resolved:** Basalt's branching pattern remains hypothetical; roof thickness above the two passage forks, lava-tube cross-sections, cave-water source, and shoulder/headroom clearance at every 2.5-foot pinch need architectural checking. No physical print or tabletop playtest has been performed. Both drawings are incomplete; real cave survey conventions are references, not provenance for these fictional dimensions.

**Next research:** Compare actual measured floor-plan-plus-profile pairs from a karst branching system and one surveyed lava tube; verify that the preserved rock ribs have meaningful thickness in transverse section. Then print at true scale and test mixed assemblies for meaningful navigation choices.
